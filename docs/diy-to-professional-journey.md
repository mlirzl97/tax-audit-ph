# DIY-to-professional product journey

## Product promise

The free taxpayer workspace must solve a real problem without requiring a
professional: calm the first-hour panic, preserve the record, organize the
case, and show the next factual task. Professional help appears when the facts
create legal, deadline, evidentiary, or representation risk—not because the
product withholds basic organization features.

## Two connected surfaces

### Free DIY workspace

1. **Triage:** ask whether an officer is on site, a subpoena or collection
   notice arrived, or a deadline may be immediate. Urgent answers stop the
   ordinary flow and recommend prompt human review.
2. **Identify:** record the notice title, actual receipt date, proof of receipt,
   taxpayer alias, and issuing office. “I am not sure” remains a valid answer.
3. **Protect:** produce a notice-specific preservation checklist and chronology.
4. **Organize:** capture each requested record or alleged discrepancy separately.
5. **Reconcile:** connect each allegation to return, books, source document,
   explanation, and evidence status without drafting legal conclusions.
6. **Prepare:** export a clean case summary, missing-items list, questions for an
   adviser, and evidence index.

The free workspace does not confirm deadlines, assess notice validity, select a
remedy, promise liability, submit a response, or represent the taxpayer.

### Professional workspace

A taxpayer can invite a CPA or lawyer into the existing case with explicit
consent. The professional receives the organized facts, chronology, missing
records, discrepancy matrix, provenance, and activity history instead of asking
the taxpayer to start again.

Professional-only actions include confirming deadlines and service, recording
the legal basis and rule version, approving reconciliations, signing off on a
response package, and recording submission or representation events.

## Evidence-based escalation

The interface shows **“Professional review recommended”** with specific reasons
when any of these are present:

- officer on site, subpoena, mission order, collection action, or enforcement;
- PAN, FLD/FAN, FDDA, or another stage where protest rights may be affected;
- unverified receipt or service, conflicting dates, or a near-term deadline;
- missing books, reconstructed records, altered records, or suspected fraud;
- prescription, authority-validity, constitutional, or criminal questions;
- material unreconciled amounts or a response intended for filing;
- the taxpayer asks the system to make a legal conclusion.

The message explains what the professional must decide. It must not manufacture
fear, hide already-created work, or claim that professional engagement is the
only possible next step.

## Interaction direction

- Taxpayer view: calm, plain-language, one question at a time, mobile first.
- Firm view: dense, scannable, multi-case dashboard with assignments and gates.
- Shared case: the same underlying record and audit trail, with role-specific
  controls rather than duplicated cases.
- Memorable detail: a **case readiness rail** showing Facts → Records →
  Reconciliation → Review → Response, with every incomplete or reviewed state
  visible and explainable.

## Reference decisions

Useful patterns observed in the Google AI Studio reference:

- guided intake leading directly into a case dossier;
- evidence checklist, source provenance, stage tabs, and activity history;
- explicit self-service assignment and professional-review status;
- separate internal dossier and external response output.

Changes required before adoption:

- remove premature firm terminology and dashboard density from taxpayer intake;
- never show an automatically calculated date as the apparent due date;
- replace hard-coded legal conclusions with versioned, reviewed rule packages;
- make the handoff actionable with a visible reason, consent, and invitation;
- persist cases securely instead of relying on local browser sample state;
- keep sample entities and alleged amounts clearly separate from real work.
